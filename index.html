Why Prism 5 Labs exists

It didn’t start as a business. It started as one man’s way to quiet his own head.

I’m Neil Saindon. I have ADHD — and I didn’t know it until I was 40.

Like everyone who goes undiagnosed that long, I built coping mechanisms. Mine ran on technology. I’ve always been hooked on scope — not how a thing works, but how much more it lets you do — so I pushed every tool I had to its fullest, just to keep the noise down.

Because the noise is the problem. The inbox, the phone, the calendar, the threads, the reminders — everything shouting at once, and none of it sorting itself. Most of the day went to handling it, and the work I actually wanted to do got whatever was left. Underneath it all: the anxiety, and the feeling of being overwhelmed that never quite clocks out.

When AI got good enough, I built the coping mechanism I’d always wanted: Prism — an assistant that handles the noise quietly in the background and brings me only what needs me. It worked. My head got quieter. And it turned out I wasn’t the only one drowning — these days, that’s almost everyone.

My coping mechanism and the advances in AI created a coping mechanism for everyone. It’s designed to reduce the noise so you can breathe, think, relax, focus — and stop worrying and feeling overwhelmed.— NEIL SAINDON, FOUNDER, IN HIS OWN WORDS

So in 2026 I founded Prism 5 Labs to build her for other people. The company is its own thing — the assistant I built for my own brain, now built for yours. If your head works like mine — too many tabs open, every one of them urgent — this was made by somebody who gets it, because he lives it.

You'll see “Est. 1955” around here. No, not really — I was born in 1984. We just design like it's 1955 — clean, optimistic, built to help — and build like it's now.

Understand our pricing — tap to open

First, the plain version. You are not buying software off a shelf. You are buying an assistant that has already been built around a real working day, tested until it works, and kept working after it arrives. That is what the price pays for: the building, the testing, and someone standing behind it afterwards.

What are the pieces?

  • A Spark is one button for one job. Push it and the job gets done. Push it again tomorrow and it gets done again. It never wears out and it is never used up. You pay once, and it is yours to keep using.
  • A Plug is a Spark with no button to push. It watches for the moment the job needs doing and does it by itself. You pay once, and it is yours to keep using.
  • A Supernova is a whole assistant — a Launch — that handles your whole front office: inbox, calendar, messages, follow-ups and paperwork. Our twelve ready-made Supernovas each carry their own name and focus; pick by the job, not the picture.
  • A Satellite is a whole assistant built around one single job — say, keeping the inbox sorted — and it does that one job every day.
  • Upkeep is a small monthly amount, and only for folks who bought outright. It covers fixes, upgrades, new jobs added, and priority help. It is never required — skip it and what you bought keeps running as installed. It is already included in every monthly plan, never charged on top.

How can I pay? Four ways — your choice, and you can ask us which fits best:

  • Pay once. One payment and it is yours outright, for good. Nothing is ever paused, and there is nothing more to pay unless you add something new.
  • Pay monthly. A smaller amount each month, with Upkeep included. If you stop paying, the living service pauses until you start again — your installed copy stays exactly as it stands, nothing is deleted and nobody chases you.
  • Pay monthly for 2 years. The same monthly plan at a lower price, because you are committing to two years. If you leave early, you only repay the discount you received — $10 a month for a Supernova, $7 a month for a Satellite, times the months used.
  • Pay for a year up front. 12 months for the price of 11 — Supernova $539 a year, Satellite $363 a year. Cancel and unused full months are refunded.

A small extra at checkout. The card company charges a 3% processing fee, and that is added when you pay — it is not hidden in the prices above. So a Supernova bought outright comes to $514 in total, and a Supernova year paid up front comes to $555.17 for the year.

Try it before you spend anything. Every Launch starts with a 14-day free trial: one real job, on your real work. No card, no charge, no auto-renew — on day 14 it stops unless you say go.

Why does it cost what it does? Because the work is already done. A Supernova takes roughly 300 hours of careful building and testing before it ever reaches you. A Satellite takes about 150 hours, a Plug about 50, a Spark about 25. You are paying for something finished and proven — not for a promise, and not for a monthly bill that never ends.

So — what exactly does Prism 5 Labs do?

Prism 5 Labs builds personal AI assistants that do routine office work — inbox, calendar, follow-ups, paperwork — and report back what’s done and what needs you. The numbers: Look at the numbers.
